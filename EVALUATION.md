# Evaluation worksheet for the Access Conversation prototype

This worksheet is a reusable starting point for a reviewed pilot, not a validated instrument or study protocol. Agree on tasks, recruitment, consent, data handling, and approvals before involving participants.

## Scenario

Use a fictional course situation. Ask the participant to explain a barrier, prepare a conversation draft, and locate the official resource or person they would consult next.

## Observe

| Question | Suggested evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Can the person complete the task? | Completed draft and correct next step, with assistance recorded. |
| Does the draft reflect their own meaning? | Participant edits, deletions, and a short explanation in their words. |
| Do they understand the tool's limits? | Ask who decides accommodations and whether a message has been sent. |
| Can they find the official source? | Observe whether they locate and interpret the linked UF page. |
| Is the interface usable? | Keyboard-only path, focus visibility, zoom, mobile layout, screen-reader labels, and error recovery. |
| What effort does it create or remove? | Ask about fatigue, cognitive load, and whether a shorter path would help. |

## Record without overclaiming

Capture the scenario version, source URLs and review date, task outcome, points of confusion, participant suggestions, and any staff review needed. Aggregate findings only under the applicable research and privacy rules. Do not infer clinical outcomes, accommodation eligibility, or institutional effectiveness from a small demonstration.

## Before any AI-enabled expansion

Decide who curates sources, how unsupported answers are handled, which data may be sent to which model, whether a text-only equivalent is available, and how people can correct or decline generated text. Compare an AI-assisted version with this transparent template before claiming the AI adds value.
